# Evaluation Scoring Guide

One page, applied each time a coach evaluates a Trackman (or radar) session against the ladder.

## The Process

### 1. Find the row
Match the pitcher's age (Youth/HS) or division (College) to the matching Level row in Velocity Ranges. If the row has separate RHP/LHP columns (most of College), use the pitcher's hand specifically. Do not read a righty benchmark onto a lefty or vice versa. Most Youth/HS rows only have Combined Low/High, since the sources behind them did not split by hand.

### 2. Classify sitting velocity (the Average from the report)
This is the number that drives the placement, not peak and not low. Compare it against the row's Low-High band:

| Where sitting velo falls | Call it |
|---|---|
| Below the row's Low | Below average for level |
| Inside Low-High, bottom half | Average — lower half of range |
| Inside Low-High, top half | Average — upper half of range |
| Above the row's High | Above average for level |

Use the band's midpoint to call upper or lower half. That is a fair read of a range, not invented precision.

### 3. Outlier tiers
There are two different ways to call "outlier," depending on what the row has:

- **13U-18U rows with an Elite Trajectory Ref filled in:** sitting velocity at or above that number is **Outlier — elite trajectory territory**. This is a sourced number (historical teenage velocity of pitchers who reached MLB), so it is a stronger claim than "above average."
- **Every other row** (College, pre-13 youth, and pro tiers once populated; no Elite Trajectory Ref exists): there is no percentile ceiling to point to, so outlier status is a judgment call. Suggested default: **3+ mph above the row's High**. Adjustable. Say so whenever you use it, since it is not a scientific cutoff.
- **On the low end, do not use "outlier."** Use **"notably behind for level"** instead. Several sources (Coleman especially) are explicit that sitting below the typical range is not a red flag by itself. Late development is normal, and "outlier" language risks pathologizing it.

### 4. Peak and Low are narrative, not classification
- **High minus Average is large:** untapped ceiling, or inconsistent effort/mechanics. Worth a projectability note, not a different tier.
- **Average minus Low is large:** fatigue or conditioning flag across the outing. Not a talent signal.

### 5. Always report the row's Confidence
Carry the Confidence rating (High/Medium/Low/Unverified) from Velocity Ranges into the write-up every time. "Above average for 16U" means something different resting on a High-confidence row than on D1 Low-Major (Unverified, one screenshot). Never let the placement read more certain than the row backing it.

## Worked Examples

**16-year-old RHP, Trackman: Low 74 / Avg 79 / High 83**
Row: 16U (HS JV/Soph). Combined 69-85, Elite Trajectory Ref 89, Confidence: Medium.
Sitting 79 falls inside 69-85, in the upper half (midpoint 77). Call: **Average — upper half of range (Medium confidence)**. Peak of 83 is 4 mph above sitting, worth noting as a projectability signal. Not an outlier: 79 is well under the 89 mph Elite Trajectory Ref.

**College sophomore RHP, JUCO, Trackman: Low 84 / Avg 88 / High 91**
Row: JUCO. RHP 82-90, Confidence: Medium.
Sitting 88 falls inside 82-90, upper half (midpoint 86). Call: **Average — upper half of range (Medium confidence)**. Peak 91 is 3 mph above sitting and 1 mph above the row's High of 90. That is a mild ceiling signal, but well short of the 93 mph line (High + 3) where the outlier buffer would apply, so no outlier flag.

## Limitation to disclose every time
True percentile-based outlier detection is only defensible for 13U and 18U, where Eisenmann's percentile data lives. Everywhere else, "outlier" is a reasoned call against a typical-range band, not a statistical claim. Say so in the write-up. It is the difference between a useful coaching tool and overselling what the data supports.
